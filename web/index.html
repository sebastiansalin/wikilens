SHOW YOUR WORK

Color, backed by revisions.

This prototype measures observed editing conflict. It does not judge whether an article is true, neutral, or socially controversial.

1 / Detect exact restorations

A revision restores an earlier SHA-1 text hash, different from the preceding revision. We attribute the event to the restoring editor and preceding editor; self-restorations are excluded. This conservative baseline misses partial reversals and cannot fully attribute multi-edit restores.

2 / Find mutual reversals

Count events only when the two editors reversed each other in both directions within the selected window. All saved revisions form the denominator. Minor edits are retained because the minor flag is not a reliable formatting classifier.

3 / Smooth the rate

Intensity = 100 × (m + 20p₀) / (N + 20)

m = mutual-reversal events; N = revisions; p₀ = the pooled rate across this sample and window. This is our proposed adaptation, not a published controversy probability. Below 20 revisions the node is gray. Fixed color scale: green at 0%, amber at 2%, red at 4% or more. Flames require ≥3% intensity and ≥5 mutual events.

Historical conflict volume

Volume = E × Σ min(nᵤ, nᵥ)

E counts participants in mutual reversals. n counts each editor’s article revisions observed by the selected date. The sum is over mutual-reversal events in the window. This adapts Yasseri et al.; it omits their strongest-pair exclusion and should not be described as an exact reproduction. Volume colors use a fixed logarithmic scale, 0–100,000.

Scope & limitations

36 deliberately selected articles from M shard 01, not a representative sample. Only links between sampled pages are shown. Titles are matched directly; redirects and renamed targets can be missed. No automatic vandalism classifier or bot filter is applied. No mutual reversals detected does not mean no dispute occurred. Silence may also reflect page protection or missing/suppressed revisions.

Node size scales with wikitext size in bytes, using bounded radii. Edge width follows the smaller endpoint’s displayed radius, capped for readability. Flames are a visual threshold, not a validated category. Graph proximity is a layout effect; edges indicate hyperlinks, not conflict spreading.
